## Missed points

- Timing simulation as a cross-check on constraints: violations reported in SDF-annotated simulation but not by static timing analysis mean the design was not constrained properly (for example a declared false path that is not one), and the flow must be rerun (§SDF Annotated GL Simulation using CVC).
- Wires contribute to delay: a wire has both resistance and capacitance, and both grow with wire length; treating a wire as a single capacitance is only valid for short wires without vias (§Modeling CMOS Cells).
- Timing analysis right after synthesis can still report setup violations even though it reports no hold violations (§Tips, tip 3). The listed pages mention only the hold half.
- Meaning of the secondary checks: a high net capacitance indicates high fanout and/or a long interconnect, and a high transition time indicates a high capacitive load and leads to more short-circuit power and delay (§Tips, tip 10).
- Limits of the automatic post-global-routing optimization and of the single-clock assumption: optimization after global routing is described as an experimental OpenROAD feature that may crash, and designs with several clock domains require the designer to adjust the constraints (§Tips, configuration table and tip 2). The pages state the single-clock assumption but not the designer's responsibility or the experimental status.
